The argument

AI adoption is usually described in abstract language: productivity, automation, leverage, agents. But people do not encounter AI abstractly. They encounter it as tabs, subscriptions, folders, prompts, screenshots, browser extensions, half-finished dashboards, and tools they keep returning to.

This series treats the desktop as a site of evidence. What remains open, what gets paid for, what gets copied, what gets abandoned, and what becomes infrastructure all reveal more than a clean list of tools.

What it studies

  1. Usefulness after novelty: what survives real work once the hype fades.
  2. Personal infrastructure: how people turn tools into systems, memory, and recurring rituals.
  3. Lineage: who people learn from, copy, cite, and remix.
  4. Taste: how the same tools produce different workflows depending on the person using them.
  5. Voyeurism: the consensual pleasure of seeing the actual setup, not the sanitized self-report.

The premise

A desktop is a self-portrait. The wallpaper, dock, tabs, tools, folders, notes, and desk objects show how someone thinks, avoids, decorates, optimizes, remembers, performs, and copes.

The series begins with Saachi's own setup, then maybe expands to other people, especially women whose digital systems are more interesting than a normal tech-stack interview can capture.
