# Series Cover Notes

## Working Title

What's On My Desktop

## Subtitle

Portraits of people through their desktops, desk setups, AI tools, tabs, prompts, wallpapers, rituals, and tiny digital dependencies.

## Inspiration Lineage

This series is an ode to:

- Glossier Top Shelf
- Gabriele Galimberti's Toy Stories
- Myspace Top 8
- desk setup tours
- women's digital rituals
- cute annotated screenshots
- personal systems that are more revealing than resumes

## Cover Premise

A desktop is a self-portrait. The wallpaper, dock, tabs, tools, folders, notes, and desk objects show how someone thinks, avoids, decorates, optimizes, remembers, performs, and copes.

## Why Saachi Thought This Was Cool

The original idea was not "write a tech stack roundup." It was:

What if an AI tech stack was treated like a Glossier bathroom cabinet, a Toy Stories inventory, a Myspace Top 8, and a desk setup tour all at once?

The fun is that AI tools have become personal objects. People have daily drivers, emotional support tabs, tools they quit, tools they pay for reluctantly, and private little workflows they would never put on a resume.

The series works because it turns AI adoption into a portrait.

It also works because people are voyeuristic. They want to see the tabs, wallpaper, folders, screenshots, receipts, and tiny systems someone actually lives with. The format should feel like a consensual peek inside a working life.
