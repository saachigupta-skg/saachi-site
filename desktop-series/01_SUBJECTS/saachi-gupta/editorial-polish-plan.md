# Saachi Profile Editorial Polish Plan

Goal: make the pilot amazing, thorough, rigorous, sparkly, fun, easy to read, engaging, and educational.

## What The Reader Should Learn

1. What Saachi actually uses.
2. What each tool is for.
3. When each layer entered the stack.
4. What survived hype.
5. What Saachi copied and who she learned from.
6. What she built because she needed it.
7. What the stack says about her.

## Page Rhythm

Use the Into The Gloss Top Shelf structure as the main editorial grammar:

1. Breadcrumb / category line.
2. Title and subject identity.
3. Long numbered photo carousel with zoom/detail images.
4. Then the interview/article sections.

For Saachi:

1. Breadcrumb: `What's On My Desktop / Saachi Gupta`.
2. Title: `Saachi Gupta, AI Stack Pilot`.
3. Numbered photo carousel: desk hero, wall layer, public surface, AI/artifact layer, analog layer, outdoor layer, screenshots.
4. Essay sections: The Stack, What I Use It For, Adoption Timeline, Screenshot Receipts, Stories Behind The Stack, Who I Learn From, Human Part.

## Tone Rules

- Earnest, not ironic.
- Sparkly, not gimmicky.
- Useful, not productivity-bro.
- Specific, not generic.
- Educational through evidence, not lectures.
- Cute annotations should make the seriousness easier to approach.

## Editorial Devices

- "Joined the party" dates for tools and desk details.
- Usefulness status: daily, weekly, experimental, abandoned, hype but useful.
- Receipts: screenshots beside claims.
- Origin stories: why a tool or system exists.
- Lineage: who taught/inspired the pattern.
- Human part: what AI does not replace.
- Numbered photo carousel with "zoom" behavior, inspired by ITG.

## Needed Content Inputs

- Exact monthly paid tools and costs.
- Which tools are daily vs weekly vs experimental.
- Which tools you quit.
- Which tools are hype but still useful.
- Which screenshots can be public.
- Your real repeated prompts.
- Your most embarrassing but useful AI use case.
